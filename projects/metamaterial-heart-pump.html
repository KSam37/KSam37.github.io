System ID of a Metamaterial Soft Robotic Ventricle

A 3D-printed metamaterial ventricle can pump like a heart — but to control it like one, you first have to identify the system. For MIT's 2.131 (Advanced Instrumentation & Measurement), our team instrumented a soft robotic ventricle in a mock circulatory loop. My contribution: replacing the pressure-controlled vacuum drive with a servo-driven hydraulic piston pump, turning an unmeasurable pneumatic input into a displacement-controlled one.

1.0The System Under Test

The plant is a soft robotic ventricle printed in Formlabs Elastic 50A, developed in prior work from the Roche lab. A metamaterial lattice forms the ventricle wall: evacuating the actuator cavity between the lattice and the inner membrane squeezes the ventricle cavity and ejects fluid, mimicking systole. The ventricle sits in a mock circulatory flow loop — compliance chambers and resistances standing in for arterial and venous beds — so its input/output behavior maps onto the equivalent circuit cardiologists use to model the real thing.

2.0Instrumentation

We instrumented the loop end-to-end: PendoTECH pressure transducers at the actuator and circulatory loop inputs and outputs, read through NAU7802 24-bit ADCs (gain 128 on a 0–25 mV bridge signal) multiplexed over I²C into an Arduino Nano Every at 320 samples per second, plus Transonic perivascular flow sensors on the inflow and outflow lines. Every claim about the ventricle's dynamics traces back to a calibrated measurement chain.

3.0My Contribution — the Hydraulic Piston Drive

The original drive was pneumatic: a vacuum reservoir, regulator, and solenoid valve pulsing the actuator cavity. For system identification that architecture has a fundamental flaw — air is compressible, so you can command pressure but you can never know the volume you delivered, and input flow can't be measured accurately. It also topped out around 30 L/min and couldn't run continuously.

I designed and built the replacement: a servo-driven hydraulic piston pump. A NEMA 23 integrated servo (iSV57T-130S) drives an SFU1605 ball-screw linear stage, which strokes an SC80 pneumatic cylinder repurposed as a water piston, all mounted on a 2020 T-slot frame. Because water is incompressible, commanded displacement is delivered volume — a one-to-one mapping from motor position to input volume that makes the input side of the system ID exactly known.

  • Displacement control instead of pressure control — the input becomes a known volume waveform.
  • 50 L/min flow capability, up from ~30 L/min with the vacuum system.
  • Continuous operation — no reservoir to deplete, so long identification runs and fatigue cycling are possible.
  • Sized from torque-speed curves against the pressure-volume demands of the ventricle; components selected and integrated from BOM to working hardware.

4.0Why It Matters

Soft robotic cardiac simulators are only useful to clinicians and device designers if their dynamics are characterized — a ventricle you can't model is a demo, not an instrument. Volume-resolved actuation closes that gap: with known input volume and measured pressures and flows on both sides, the identification pipeline (implemented in MATLAB) can fit the equivalent-circuit parameters of the ventricle and loop. The same lesson generalizes across soft robotics: choose the actuation modality that makes your states observable.
